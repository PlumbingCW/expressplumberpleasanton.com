Emergency sewer line cleaning in Pleasanton begins when the lowest drain in the house starts to fill. A tub that rises as the washer drains, a toilet that bubbles when a sink empties, or a floor drain that weeps are all the same warning. The main line is blocked, and every fixture upstream is waiting its turn. Call (510) 544-1962 at any hour.

Stop the washing machine and dishwasher, keep showers off, and avoid flushing. If a cleanout cap is outside, leave it closed until we arrive unless you are told otherwise. A full line under pressure can spray when the cap opens.

Sewer backup: what to do first

Do these before the plumber arrives

Shut off water use in the house and ask everyone to stay out of the affected room. Move rugs, electronics and cardboard away from the drain. Do not use a wet vacuum in standing sewage. Photograph the water level and any damage for your insurance record, and wash your hands after contact.

If sewage has reached a wall outlet, appliance or electrical panel area, leave that room alone and call us. Power and water are a dangerous mix, and a plumber should assess the room before anyone steps in.

Where Pleasanton's sewer responsibility changes

The City of Pleasanton maintains its trunk sewers. Its Public Works customer service page cites Section 15.32.100 to say that side sewers and service laterals are the property owner's responsibility. Wastewater from Pleasanton is treated by the Dublin San Ramon Services District under contract, but the pipe in your yard is yours.

If neighbors are also backing up, the main in the street may be the cause. Call the city line at (925) 931-5500 on weekdays or Pleasanton Police Dispatch at (925) 931-5100 after hours, as listed on the city's page. If the main is flowing, we clear your lateral, then camera it.

Cleanouts: the single best feature a lateral can have

A cleanout is a capped access pipe that lets us put a cable or camera straight into the lateral. With one, a main line stoppage is a short visit. Without one, we may have to remove a toilet or work from the roof vent, which takes longer and costs more.

If your house has no accessible cleanout, we will tell you where one should go. Permit rules vary by city, so we confirm with the building department and pull a permit when one applies.

Insurance and backups

Water damage from a sewer backup is often excluded from a standard homeowner policy and covered only by an added endorsement. Check your declarations page, and tell your insurer promptly if damage occurred. We can provide a written description of the cause and the work.
