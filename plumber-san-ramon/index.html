Emergency plumbing in San Ramon means a plumber from Pleasanton heading north on I-680, about ten miles and fifteen minutes in normal traffic. Our 24/7 line at (510) 544-1962 answers any hour, and the dispatcher gives you an arrival window before the truck leaves. San Ramon straddles two ZIP codes, 94582 and 94583, and we serve both.

The calls here fall into a few patterns. This page sorts them by what is going wrong, because that is how people describe it on the phone.

Water is coming through a ceiling or wall

A burst supply line, a failed water heater or a leaking fixture upstairs is the most urgent call we get in San Ramon. San Ramon has an average annual rainfall near 26 inches, but a rain-free week is no protection against an indoor failure. Shut off the main, open a low tap to relieve pressure and call.

Our burst pipe repair page walks through the first ten minutes. If the leak is slow rather than sudden, leak detection finds it before the drywall does.

The office or restaurant is down

Bishop Ranch is a master-planned office park that began major construction in the early 1980s and covers most of central San Ramon. City Center Bishop Ranch opened in November 2018 with shops, restaurants, a movie theater and an ice rink. Those buildings put heavy loads on restrooms, kitchens and water heaters.

A backed-up restroom or a failed commercial heater stops a business in hours. Our commercial plumbing team works nights and weekends so tenants can stay open, and we write up what failed for the property manager.

Hot water has stopped or the bill has jumped

A heater that fails on a cold morning is a close second to a burst pipe. The cure depends on age, fuel and what the tank looks like inside, and the repair-or-replace table on our water heater repair page covers the decision. If a heater is leaking from the tank body, replacement is the only option.

A bill that jumps with no change in use points to a hidden leak. Run the meter test on our leak detection page, and call if the meter keeps moving with every fixture off.

Who supplies your water in San Ramon

San Ramon is served by more than one agency. Dublin San Ramon Services District provides water in the eastern part of the city in the Dougherty Valley, and EBMUD's boundaries include portions of San Ramon. For wastewater, DSRSD serves the southern part of the city and Central San serves a portion of it.

Your bill shows which agency you belong to. Each publishes a water quality report, and the right one tells you more about your tap than a general map can.

Before we arrive in San Ramon

STEP 1
