What a Birdland address tells us, and what it does not

A single-story ranch usually has its water heater in a garage or a closet and its main shutoff near the front or side of the house. That layout makes a leak at the heater or a hose bib easy to find once you know where to look.

A neighborhood name does not tell us what pipe is in your walls. Some Birdland owners have replaced supply lines, drains or fixtures over the years, and some have not, so we inspect your house before we recommend anything. Nobody should sell you a repipe because of a street name.

Plumbing calls we take in Birdland

  • Drain cleaning for kitchen sinks, tubs and floor drains that back up or drain slowly.
  • Water heater repair when the tank leaks, the pilot fails or the water runs lukewarm.
  • Leak detection when the water bill climbs and no faucet is dripping.
  • Toilet repair for running tanks, weak flushes and rocking bases.
  • Sewer line cleaning when more than one fixture backs up at the same time.

Pleasanton's city water report lists hardness between 100 and 450 mg/L depending on the source, so scale on fixtures and inside water heater tanks is common across town. A Birdland tank that rumbles or heats slowly may simply need a flush before anyone talks about replacement.

Before the plumber arrives

If water is running in your Birdland home

  1. Close the main shutoff, usually near the front of the house or at the meter box by the curb.
  2. Open the lowest faucet to drain the lines and slow the flow.
  3. Switch off the water heater if the tank itself is leaking.
  4. Call (510) 544-1962 and give us the cross streets, so we can find the house quickly.

What we cannot confirm

We could not find a public source that states which pipe materials, sewer lateral ages or water retailer apply to individual Birdland streets. If you want those details, your water bill, your home inspection report and the city permit history for your address are the places to look.
