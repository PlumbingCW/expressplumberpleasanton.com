Emergency burst pipe repair in Pleasanton begins with the shutoff. Water under pressure can fill a room in minutes, so the first job is to stop it and the second is to call (510) 544-1962. A plumber can then repair the pipe, test the line and tell you what to dry out.

What to do in the first ten minutes

Stop the water, protect the house

  1. Close the main shutoff valve at the house. If you cannot find it or it will not turn, close the valve at the meter with a meter key or pliers.
  2. Turn off the water heater, since a heater without incoming water can overheat.
  3. Open the lowest faucet in the house and flush a toilet to drain remaining pressure.
  4. Switch off power to any room with standing water near outlets, only if you can reach the panel without stepping in water.
  5. Move furniture and rugs, photograph the damage, then call.

Keep a tool near the main shutoff so it is ready. Many homeowners learn where the valve is only when the pipe fails.

Why pipes burst in the Tri-Valley

Hard freezes are uncommon here, so ice is rarely the culprit. Most bursts come from corrosion, an old fitting, high supply pressure or a pipe that has been rubbing against a stud or a neighboring line. Cold snaps do happen occasionally, and pipes in attics, garages and exterior walls are the exposed ones on those nights.

High pressure is a hidden cause. A pressure regulator that has failed lets the full street pressure into your home, and fittings give way. We test pressure at every burst pipe call.

Repair or replace the whole line?

One split in an otherwise good line is a repair. A line that has already failed in more than one place, or galvanized pipe with rusty water, is a candidate for replacement. Our repiping page explains when a larger job saves money over repeated emergency calls.

If the burst is in the buried line to the street, the work is different. See water line repair for how that is handled.

After the pipe is fixed

What to do once the water is off and the pipe is repaired

  • Photograph and list damaged items before anything is thrown out.
  • Call your insurer the same day and ask about mitigation steps they require.
  • Start drying with fans and open windows, and call a restoration company if walls or floors are wet.
  • Watch for musty smells and discoloration over the next few days.
  • Keep every receipt for temporary housing, drying and repair work.

Why speed matters for mold

Wet drywall and insulation can start to grow mold in a day or two, depending on conditions. Moving air and removing soaked material quickly lowers that risk.
