Danville's plumbing calls follow the weather and the age of the neighborhood. Here is how the seasons and the housing eras line up.

Wet season: November through March

The San Ramon Valley gets its heaviest rain from about November through March. That is when we see slow main drains turn into backups, because roots find the joints and heavy rain loads the system. A toilet that gurgles or a floor drain that bubbles while the shower runs is a warning.

If more than one fixture is slow, the blockage is in the main. A sewer line cleaning visit with a camera shows the cause, and hydro jetting clears grease and roots from the pipe wall.

Dry season: slab, supply and sprinkler leaks

Summer is when hidden leaks show up as bills. Irrigation lines, outdoor hose bibs and buried supply pipes run hard, and a leak under a yard stays out of sight. Sycamore Creek drains the slopes of Mount Diablo and runs through town, but a patch of soggy ground far from the creek is more likely a pipe.

The meter test in our leak detection guide is a five-minute check. If the meter moves with everything off, call us.

Older streets and the 1970s and 1980s boom

Danville began developing as a residential suburb after 1947, when the first sizable housing tracts were built. Developments such as Montair and Cameo Acres followed in the 1940s and 1950s. I-680 came through in the mid-1960s, and the population climbed from about 14,000 in 1970 to more than 26,000 in 1980.

That means many Danville homes are between forty and seventy-five years old. Their original sewer laterals may be clay or cast iron, and mature trees in established lots reach for any joint that weeps. Our sewer line repair page covers what is possible when the pipe has failed.
