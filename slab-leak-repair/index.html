Emergency slab leak repair in Pleasanton starts with proof. Many homes here sit on a concrete foundation with supply lines running beneath it, and when one of those lines fails the water has nowhere obvious to go. It can lift flooring, soak the base of walls and drive a water bill up fast. If you suspect one, close the main valve and call (510) 544-1962.

Signs of a leak beneath the foundation

What homeowners notice first

  • A warm or hot spot on the floor, often where a hot water line runs.
  • The sound of water running when every fixture is off.
  • A water meter that moves with the whole house shut down.
  • Damp carpet, buckling wood floors or wet baseboards with no visible source.
  • A sudden drop in water pressure or a hot water heater that runs constantly.

Any one of these signs can have another cause, which is why we confirm before cutting. A leak detection visit tells us whether the problem is under the slab, in a wall or in the yard.

Why under-slab pipes fail

Copper under a slab is surrounded by soil and concrete, which can wear on it from the outside as well as the inside. Aggressive water chemistry, a high flow velocity and a pipe rubbing against concrete or a neighboring line can all start a pinhole.

Soil plays a part too. Clay and silt soils like those documented in Pleasanton's Gates neighborhood swell and shrink with the seasons, and a slab that shifts even slightly pulls at the pipes below it.

Your repair options

Three ways to fix a slab leak.
OptionHow it worksBest when
Spot repairWe open the slab over the leak, replace the damaged section and patch the concreteOne leak on an otherwise sound line.
RerouteWe abandon the failed line and run a new one through the attic or wallsRepeated leaks or hard-to-reach pipe.
RepipeReplace the whole supply system with new pipeAging copper with multiple failures.

Spot repair is quickest and lowest in cost, but it only fixes the spot. If the home has had more than one slab leak, a reroute or a repipe is usually the longer-term answer, and we show you the numbers for each.

What the work looks like

The usual sequence

  1. We shut off the water, confirm the leak with a pressure test and mark its location.
  2. We protect the floors and set up dust control before opening the slab.
  3. The section is cut, the pipe is repaired or replaced and the repair is pressure tested.
  4. Concrete is patched and the area is left ready for your flooring contractor.
  5. We leave you a written note of the leak location and repair for your insurer and for future owners.

If the leak has soaked flooring or drywall, drying is a separate trade. We tell you when to call a restoration company and what to document first.

Slab leaks, bills and insurance

A slab leak can run for weeks before anyone knows. The City of Pleasanton's leak adjustment form says an adjustment may be available when use exceeds three times your average and the leak is on your side of the meter, with a repair invoice as proof. Requests are due within 30 days of the billing date, and only one adjustment is allowed in 36 months. We provide a written leak report.

Many homeowner policies cover resulting water damage and sometimes the access cost to reach the pipe, but not the pipe itself. Read your policy and call your insurer before work starts.
