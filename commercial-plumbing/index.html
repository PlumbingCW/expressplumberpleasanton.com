Emergency Commercial Plumbing in Pleasanton, CA

When a restroom backs up or a line breaks during business hours, every hour costs money. We respond to offices, restaurants, retail and multi-unit properties around Pleasanton, day or night.

Emergency commercial plumbing in Pleasanton is a different job from a house call. Pipes are larger, fixtures are shared, and a failure can close a restroom, a kitchen or a whole suite. We work with property managers, tenants and owners across the city, and we keep after-hours visits for exactly these calls. Call (510) 544-1962 and tell us the building type.

Offices, retail and multi-tenant buildings

Pleasanton's commercial areas include the Hacienda Business Park and the retail district around Stoneridge Shopping Center. Buildings in those areas range from newer office campuses to older retail strips, and each has different plumbing. Property managers often need a written record of what failed, where and when, and we provide one.

In multi-tenant buildings, we also help work out which side of a shared line the blockage sits on. That is a question tenants and landlords often need answered before deciding who pays.

Restaurants, grease and drains

Food service puts heavy loads on drains. Grease cools and sticks to pipe walls, and a kitchen line that is not cleaned on a schedule will eventually back up in the worst moment. We clear food service lines with hydro jetting and recommend a cleaning interval based on what we find.

Grease interceptors and traps need regular pumping, and local rules apply. Ask the City of Pleasanton or Dublin San Ramon Services District about requirements for your address, and we can fit service around your schedule.

Backflow prevention and testing

Commercial buildings often have backflow prevention assemblies that protect the public water supply. These devices are usually tested on a regular schedule and must be repaired if they fail. Check with the City of Pleasanton for the testing rules that apply to your property.

If a device fails a test, repair or replacement is needed before the water can be considered protected. We tell you what failed and what it will take to pass.

Emergency response for a business

First steps when a line fails

  1. Shut off the water to the affected area or the whole building if you cannot isolate it.
  2. Move inventory, files and electronics out of reach of water.
  3. Close the area to customers and post a sign if the floor is wet.
  4. Photograph the damage and the source for your insurer and landlord.
  5. Call (510) 544-1962 and describe the building, the fixture and the shutoff you used.

Water that reaches electrical panels, server rooms or kitchen equipment is a priority. Say so on the call and we plan the response around it.
