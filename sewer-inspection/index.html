Sewer camera inspection in Pleasanton is the fastest way to turn a guess into a fact. A waterproof camera on a flexible cable goes into the cleanout and follows the lateral toward the street, recording roots, cracks, offsets, low spots and buildup. You see the video on a monitor, and you leave with notes you can use. Call (510) 544-1962 to book a visit.

Who should schedule a camera inspection

Most scopes happen for one of three reasons. A home buyer wants to know what the sewer lateral looks like before closing. An owner has had a backup and wants to know why. A homeowner with an older house and mature trees wants to catch trouble before it becomes a backup at the worst moment.

If sewage is rising right now, that is an emergency, and the first step is clearing the line. We can camera it in the same visit once it is flowing again.

What the camera looks for

Findings that matter

  • Roots entering at joints or through cracks, and how heavy the growth is.
  • Offset joints where one pipe section has shifted from the next.
  • Low spots or bellies that hold water and collect paper.
  • Cracks, fractures or missing pipe wall.
  • Scale or grease coating that narrows the pipe.
  • Connections where the lateral meets the city main.

What you get after the inspection

A written summary of what the camera saw, with the distance to each finding measured from the cleanout. We do not push a repair when the line is sound. If the line needs work, we explain the options and the trade-offs, then quote a price.

If the lateral is in good shape, a clean report is valuable. It documents the condition for your records and for any buyer later.

Local context for Pleasanton laterals

A lateral under a house near Downtown Pleasanton may be clay or cast iron from the original construction, and a lateral in a subdivision such as Callippe Preserve is more likely plastic. The camera tells you which you have. That matters because roots enter clay joints, while plastic fails differently.

Settling is the other reason to look. If a driveway slab has cracked or a patch of yard has sunk near the house, a camera pass can show whether the lateral moved with it or stayed true.

Buying a home in the Tri-Valley? Scope the lateral

A pre-purchase scope costs a small fraction of a sewer line replacement. The City of Pleasanton assigns lateral maintenance to the property owner in Section 15.32.100 of its code, as its Public Works page notes, so a buyer inherits any damage. The same split applies in Livermore, where the city says owners maintain, repair and replace their laterals.

Ask for the scope before the inspection contingency ends. If the report shows damage, the numbers from our sewer line repair page help you negotiate.
