Whole House Repiping in Pleasanton, CA

When pipes keep failing, patching them costs more over time than replacing them. We repipe homes in Pleasanton with copper or PEX and keep drywall repair to a minimum.

Whole house repiping in Pleasanton is a decision for homes where the supply pipes are failing faster than patches can fix them. The goal is to replace the old pipe with new, so you get steady pressure, clean water and fewer surprise leaks. Call (510) 544-1962 for a visit.

Signs your pipes are due for replacement

When patching stops making sense

  • Repeated pinhole leaks in copper, especially in more than one room.
  • Rust-colored water from the tap, which points to corroded galvanized pipe.
  • Low pressure at most fixtures, not just one.
  • Visible green or white mineral crust on pipe joints.
  • A home with gray plastic supply lines from the 1970s to 1990s, which may be polybutylene.

If you only have one leak and the rest of the pipe is sound, a repair is cheaper. A leak detection visit can help you decide which case you are in.

What a repipe involves

The usual sequence

  1. We inspect the existing lines, count fixtures and agree on a plan and a price.
  2. Permit is pulled when required, and your water shutoff schedule is set.
  3. New lines are run through walls, attic or crawlspace, with small access holes in drywall where needed.
  4. Fixtures are reconnected, the system is pressure tested and an inspection is scheduled.
  5. Drywall openings are patched or left ready for a finishing trade, as agreed.

The water is usually off for part of a day or longer, depending on the size of the house. We plan the sequence so you have water at night wherever possible.

Getting your home ready for a repipe

Clear access to the water heater, the main shutoff and the cabinets under sinks. Move furniture away from walls where lines will be opened, and plan for a day or two without normal water use. Arrange laundry and showers elsewhere if the schedule is tight.

Tell us about past leaks, additions or remodels, since they can mean mixed materials behind the walls. The more we know before the visit, the more accurate the price, and the fewer surprises appear once a wall is open.

Slab homes and homes with crawlspaces

Many Pleasanton homes sit on a slab with lines under the floor. In those, we often abandon the underground pipe and run new lines through the attic, which avoids breaking concrete. Homes with crawlspaces let us work from below, which keeps drywall damage low.

Older homes near Downtown Pleasanton may have a mix of materials added over decades. A repipe replaces the whole system with one material, which makes future repairs simpler.

Your permit and inspection

A repipe is major plumbing work. The City of Pleasanton's permit handout says ordinary repairs do not include replacement or relocation of water supply piping, so a repipe needs a permit. We pull the permit and meet the inspector.

A passed inspection is a record of the work that you can show buyers and insurers later.
