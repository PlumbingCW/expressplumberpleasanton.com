Emergency Toilet Repair in Pleasanton, CA

An overflowing toilet is a mess you want gone now. We stop the water, clear the clog and repair leaks, running tanks, loose bowls and failed flanges in Pleasanton.

Emergency toilet repair in Pleasanton covers three kinds of calls. These are an overflow in progress, a toilet that will not flush or keep running, and one that leaks at the base. The first is urgent, the second is a nuisance that wastes water, and the third can rot a floor if it is ignored. Call (510) 544-1962 for any of them.

Stop an overflow first

Do this before the water reaches the floor

  1. Take the tank lid off and push the flapper firmly down to stop more water entering the bowl.
  2. Turn the supply valve behind the toilet clockwise until it stops.
  3. Do not flush again. Each flush adds more water to a clogged bowl.
  4. Use towels to protect the floor, and wear gloves if the water is dirty.
  5. If other drains in the house are slow or gurgling, stop using water and call us, since the main line may be blocked.

A single toilet that clogs is usually a local problem. When several fixtures act up at once, the blockage is in the main drain, and a plunger will not reach it.

Clogs and when a plunger is not enough

Most single-toilet clogs clear with a flange plunger and steady pressure. A closet auger reaches farther and avoids scratching the bowl. If the clog keeps returning, something more is wrong, such as a toy or a wipe stuck in the trap, tree roots in the line or a partly collapsed drain.

We use a camera when a clog comes back. Seeing the pipe is faster than guessing, and it tells you if the fix is a simple clear or a repair. If you have several slow drains, drain cleaning or a sewer camera inspection is likely the better next step.

Leaks at the base and wobbly toilets

A toilet sits on a flange and a wax ring or rubber gasket seals it. If the toilet rocks, the seal breaks, and water can seep under the base and soften the subfloor. The first sign may be a stain on the ceiling below or a soft spot near the toilet.

We pull the toilet, inspect the flange, replace the seal and reset it level. If the flange is cracked or sits too low, we repair it so the new seal holds.

The City of Pleasanton's residential bathroom handout lists 1.28 gallons per flush for toilets, so a replacement often uses less water than an older unit.

The city's permit handout exempts clearing stoppages, stopping leaks and removing and reinstalling a toilet, as long as no valves, pipes or fixtures are replaced or rearranged. A typical clog or reseat needs no permit, while replacing a fixture falls outside that exemption.
