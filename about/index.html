Pleasanton Express Plumbing is a local plumbing company that answers a 24/7 emergency line and dispatches licensed and insured plumbers across Pleasanton, Livermore, Dublin, San Ramon and Danville. Our office is at 4309 Hacienda Dr, Pleasanton, CA 94588.

This page explains how we work, what we will and will not do and where our guides get their facts. We would rather you know before you call.

How we handle a call

From the first ring to the finished repair

  1. A dispatcher answers, asks what is happening and tells you what to shut off.
  2. We give you an arrival window and send the plumber who can reach you first.
  3. The plumber diagnoses the problem and explains it in plain words.
  4. You hear the price before work starts, and you decide whether to go ahead.
  5. We test the repair and tell you what to watch for afterward.

What we promise and what we do not

We promise a clear price before work starts, same-day appointments when the schedule allows and a straight answer on whether to repair or replace. We do not promise a fixed arrival minute, because traffic and call volume change, and we would rather give you an honest window than a hopeful one.

We do not use forms, pressure tactics or scare lines. If a repair is not worth the money, we tell you, even when the bigger job would pay us more.

Where our guides get their facts

Our service and blog pages cite city handouts, utility reports and agency pages, and they name the source type. For example, our water heater pages draw on the City of Pleasanton's water heater handout and the Bay Area Air Quality Management District's building appliances page. Our water quality notes draw on the City of Pleasanton's annual water quality report.

When a fact could not be verified, we left it out or told you to ask the city or your water utility. Codes change, so check the current edition with the Building and Safety Division before a project.

Questions worth asking any plumber

Whether you call us or someone else, a few questions protect you. Ask for the price before work starts, and ask what is included in it. Ask whether the repair needs a permit and who will pull it. Ask what the old part looked like and why it failed.

A good plumber answers these in plain words and does not rush you. If the answer to a repair question is always a full replacement, ask for the reason in writing or get a second opinion.

Check the license yourself

California contractors are licensed through the Contractors State License Board. Before you hire anyone, you can look up a license number on the board's website and confirm the status. Ask any company for its number up front.

What to have ready when you call

  • The address and a short description of what is happening.
  • Whether the water is still running and whether you have found the main shutoff.
  • For a water heater, the fuel type, the age and where it sits.
  • For a drain, which fixtures are slow or full and whether the problem started suddenly.

That information lets the dispatcher tell you what to do next and send the right plumber with the right parts. Nobody needs the details perfect. A rough description and an address are enough to start.

If you cannot find the main shutoff, say so. The dispatcher can describe where it usually sits, such as near the front hose bib, in a garage wall or at the water meter box by the curb.
