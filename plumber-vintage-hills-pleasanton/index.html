Hillside streets and water pressure

Water pressure in a house depends partly on its elevation relative to the supply, so a hillside street can see higher or lower pressure than a flat one. That is general plumbing knowledge, not a documented fact about any Vintage Hills street.

If your faucets hammer, your toilet fills with a bang or a supply hose has burst, a pressure test takes a few minutes. We can check the reading, look at the pressure regulator if the house has one and tell you whether it needs adjusting or replacing.

Plumbing jobs we do on these streets

  • Water heater repair for tanks that leak at the fittings, run cold or make noise.
  • Water heater installation when a tank is at the end of its life and you want a clean swap.
  • Leak detection for wet spots, warm floors or a meter that keeps turning.
  • Burst pipe repair when a line fails and the water needs to be stopped fast.
  • Plumbing installation for new fixtures during a kitchen or bath update.

A mix of builders and styles means a mix of remodel histories. A bathroom that was updated in the 1990s can sit beside original fixtures in the next room, which is one more reason we look before we quote.

What to do before we get there

STEP 1

Find the main

Close the main valve at the house or the meter. Hillside lots sometimes put the meter at the street end of a long drive.

STEP 2

Stop the heater

If the tank is leaking, switch off the gas control or the power to the water heater.

STEP 3

Catch what you can

Move rugs and furniture and put a bucket under the drip, so water does not reach the subfloor.

STEP 4

Call us

Call (510) 544-1962 and tell us the cross street and the lot access, so the plumber can park and find the door.
