Remodels and additions

A remodel is the best time to fix hidden problems. Opening a wall reveals old pipe, a weak vent or a drain with too little slope, and the cost to correct it is low while the wall is open. We coordinate with your contractor so the rough-in is inspected before drywall closes.

Moving a sink, a toilet or a shower changes the drain and vent layout, and a slab home may need concrete cut for new drain lines. We explain what each change involves before you finalize a plan.

Gas line installation and safety

Gas lines serve ranges, dryers, water heaters, fireplaces and outdoor grills. The City of Pleasanton says a plumbing trade permit is required for all new, relocated, extended or altered gas piping. Its gas piping handout calls for an accessible shutoff valve at each appliance and a rough-in pressure test at 10 psi for at least 15 minutes. We size the line for the total load and meet the inspector.

If you smell gas, leave first

Do not switch lights or use a phone inside. Leave the building, then call 911 from outside, and call PG&E's gas emergency line at 1-800-743-5000. Do not return until you are told it is safe.

Choosing and supplying fixtures

You can supply your own fixtures or have us source them. If you bring them, check the rough-in dimensions and the mounting hardware before the visit, since a toilet with the wrong rough-in will not fit. We confirm the fit when we walk the job.

For a faucet that needs repair rather than installation, see faucet repair. If you are replacing a failed unit, toilet repair and garbage disposal repair cover those.

Appliance hookups homeowners overlook

A dishwasher needs an air gap or a high loop so dirty water cannot flow back into it. A refrigerator ice maker needs a dedicated supply line with its own shutoff. A washer needs a proper standpipe, a trap and valves that close fully, since a failed hose is among the most common causes of a flooded laundry room.

We check each connection during the visit and replace rubber hoses with braided lines where it makes sense. Small upgrades like these cost little and prevent the kind of leak that gets found only after the cabinet floor has softened.
