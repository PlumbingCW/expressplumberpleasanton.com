Tankless water heater service in Pleasanton comes down to three requests: install one, fix one that misbehaves, and flush one that has been running for years. The units heat water on demand, so they depend on correct gas sizing, a proper vent and clean heat exchangers. We handle all of it. Call (510) 544-1962 to book.

Gas and vent safety

What to do first: gas appliances and vents

If you smell gas, leave the house, do not touch switches, and call 911 from outside. Then call PG&E at 1-800-743-5000. A tankless unit that shows a vent or exhaust fault code should stay off until a licensed plumber checks the vent, because blocked exhaust can let combustion gases into the home.

It runs, but the hot water is not right

Most tankless calls start with a symptom, not a model number. The cold sandwich, where the water turns hot then cold then hot again, usually comes from flow dropping below the minimum the unit needs to fire. A shower that turns lukewarm when someone runs the kitchen tap points to demand exceeding the unit's rated flow. An error code with a lockout is often a flame, vent or scale problem.

None of these means the unit is bad. They mean the unit is mismatched, dirty or poorly set up. The cure is a diagnosis, not a guess.

Flushing and descaling a tankless heater

Flush and descale is the single most common tankless service. A pump pushes a descaling solution through the heat exchanger, dissolving mineral scale that insulates the metal and cuts flow. Manufacturers generally recommend annual service in hard water areas, and some set specific intervals in the manual.

The City of Pleasanton's 2024 water quality report lists average hardness of 86 mg/L for Zone 7 treated surface water and 309 mg/L for groundwater. Where your address sits between those depends on the blend, so test your tap. A softener ahead of the heater is the surest way to slow scale, and our water softener page covers it.

Sizing: flow rate and temperature rise

A tankless unit is rated in gallons per minute at a given temperature rise. Rise is the difference between the incoming water and your set temperature. The warmer the incoming water, the higher the flow the unit can deliver. In winter, with colder incoming water, the same unit delivers less.

Count the fixtures that might run at once, add their flow rates, and compare the total with the unit's rated flow at your rise. If the two do not match, a second unit or a larger one is the fix.

Venting a tankless heater

Tankless units vent differently from a tank. Condensing models use plastic pipe that can run through a wall and need a condensate drain. Non-condensing models use stainless vent pipe and run hotter. Vent length, elbows and termination clearance are all limited by the manufacturer, and a bad vent is the leading cause of recurring lockouts.

We check vent length and clearance during any install or repair, and we tell you if a vent needs to change before the unit is reliable.
