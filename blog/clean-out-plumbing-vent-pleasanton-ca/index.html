How to Clean Out a Plumbing Vent: A Pleasanton, CA Homeowner Guide

A blocked roof vent makes drains gurgle, flush slowly and smell. Clearing it is often easy, but the roof is the real hazard, so this guide covers the safe way to do it.

Call (510) 544-1962
By the Pleasanton Express Plumbing teamUpdated November 13, 20268 min read

A plumbing vent is a pipe that runs from your drain system up through the roof. It lets air in behind water as it drains, so water flows smoothly, and it lets sewer gas escape above the house. When a vent clogs, water leaves a drain slowly, the pipes gurgle and sometimes a sewer smell appears.

Clearing a vent usually means a short trip to the roof with a garden hose or a plumber's snake. The work is simple, and the danger is falling, so please read the safety section before you climb.

Signs a vent may be blocked

What the symptom points to.
SymptomLikely causeTest
Gurgling after a toilet flush or a sink drainsAir cannot enter the pipe behind the waterRun one fixture and listen at the others.
Slow drains in several fixtures but no standing waterPartial vent blockageOpen a roof vent cap and run water.
Sewer smell in a bathroom or the yardFailed vent or dry trapCheck traps and the vent cap.
Toilet bowl water bobs up and downNegative pressure from a blocked ventWatch the bowl after a drain runs.
Backups in the lowest drainMain line blockage, not a ventTry the cleanout or call a plumber.

The last row matters. A vent problem and a main line problem can look alike, and clearing a vent will not fix a stoppage in the sewer line. If the lowest drain in the house backs up, the main is more likely the cause.

What blocks a vent

Leaves, nests and debris land in the open pipe on the roof. In Pleasanton's dry, wooded neighborhoods, such as streets near the ridge, tree litter is an ordinary cause. Birds and rodents also build nests in vent pipes, and a vent cap that is too fine can clog with debris.

Less often, a vent clogs from inside. A vent that shares a pipe with a kitchen drain can collect grease, and a poorly pitched vent in a wall can hold condensation that freezes in cold climates. Here, the issue is more likely debris than ice.

Roof safety comes first

Before you climb

  • Never go on a wet, damp or dewy roof, or in wind.
  • Use a stable ladder that extends at least three feet above the roof edge, and have someone hold it.
  • Wear shoes with grip, and use a safety harness if the pitch is steep.
  • Avoid tile and shake roofs unless you know how to walk them, since they crack and slide.
  • If you are unsure, skip it and call a plumber.

Every year people fall from roofs while doing small chores. A plumber is a reasonable cost against an injury.

Clearing the vent from above

  1. Find the vent pipe, a round pipe sticking up through the roof, often near the bathrooms or kitchen.
  2. Look inside with a flashlight and pull out visible debris with a gloved hand or a grabber.
  3. Run a garden hose down the pipe and listen for gurgling that tells you the water is draining.
  4. If the water backs up, feed a plumber's snake or a flexible auger down and work it gently to break the blockage.
  5. Flush a toilet and run a sink in the house to see whether the gurgling stops.

Where the vent ends and the city sewer begins

The City of Pleasanton's Sewer System Management Plan says the sewer system has about 250 miles of gravity sewers, roughly 25,000 feet of force main and ten pump stations. Wastewater is treated at the Dublin San Ramon Services District plant. The plan also says sewer service laterals are owned by, and the responsibility of, the property owner.

That split helps with diagnosis. A vent is part of your house plumbing. A lateral is yours too, from the house to the connection with the city main. If more than one neighbor has a backup at once, the main in the street is the likelier cause, and the city should hear about it.

Source type: City of Pleasanton 2019 Sewer System Management Plan update.

Other ways a vent fails

If the vent is clear and the gurgling continues, the problem may be inside the wall. A vent pipe can be crushed during a remodel, disconnected at a joint or sagging so it holds water. These need a camera and an access hole, and they are plumber jobs.

A dry trap is another cause of smell. A trap under a rarely used sink or floor drain dries out, and sewer gas comes up. Run water in it for a minute, and pour a little water or mineral oil in to keep the seal.

When to call a plumber

Call if you cannot reach the vent safely, if the blockage does not clear, if the main line is backing up or if you hear gurgling in several fixtures that a vent cleaning did not fix. A plumber can use a camera and a cable machine from the cleanout or the roof.

A note on air admittance valves

Some fixtures, especially under a kitchen island or in a remodeled bathroom, use an air admittance valve instead of a pipe to the roof. The valve opens to let air in as water drains and closes to keep sewer gas out. When it sticks, the fixture gurgles and drains slowly.

These valves wear out and are usually replaced rather than cleaned. A plumber can swap one in minutes, and it is a good first thing to check when a single island sink or a distant bathroom has trouble that the roof vents do not explain.
