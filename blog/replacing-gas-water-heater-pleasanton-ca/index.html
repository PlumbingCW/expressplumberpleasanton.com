Replacing a Gas Water Heater: A Pleasanton, CA Homeowner Guide

Swapping a gas water heater looks simple until the permit, the straps, the vent and the air rule come into view. Here is what to expect, and what to ask before you buy a tank.

Call (510) 544-1962
By the Pleasanton Express Plumbing teamUpdated November 13, 20269 min read

Replacing a gas water heater takes a licensed plumber about half a day when nothing goes wrong. The steps are drain, disconnect, set the new tank, reconnect water and gas, vent, test and inspect. The surprises tend to come from the vent, the gas shutoff, the relief valve drain and a missing expansion tank.

If you are deciding between doing it yourself and hiring a plumber, the short answer is to hire one for gas. A gas leak or a bad vent is a safety issue, and Pleasanton requires a permit for the work.

How to tell it is time to replace

Signs the tank is finished

  • Water seeping from the tank body or its seam, not just a fitting.
  • Rusty hot water or flakes from the hot tap.
  • A heater older than about twelve years that now needs frequent repairs.
  • Loud rumbling from sediment baked onto the tank floor.
  • A pilot or burner that will not stay lit even after a new part.

A repair costs less when the heater is young and the fault is a small part. Our water heater repair page lists the symptoms that justify a repair, and the table there helps you compare.

The first-hour rating on the yellow label is more useful than the gallon count. It tells you how much hot water the heater can deliver in the first sixty minutes of heavy use. A 40-gallon tank with a strong burner can outperform a 50-gallon tank with a weak one.

Pleasanton and Bay Area rules for a gas water heater swap

The City of Pleasanton's water heater handout says a plumbing permit is required to install, remove, relocate or replace a water heater. It calls for two seismic straps, an expansion tank when the system has a check valve or other normally closed device, and a relief valve drain that ends 6 to 24 inches above grade. In a garage, the heater must sit on an 18-inch platform unless it is flammable vapor ignition resistant.

The Bay Area Air Quality Management District page on building appliances says its board adopted amendments to Rule 9-6 on November 4, 2026, delaying the zero-NOx compliance date for residential water heaters to January 1, 2028. It says the rule applies only when a water heater is replaced and does not require replacing a working unit.

Source types: City of Pleasanton Building and Safety water heater handout (revised January 2020, 2019 California Plumbing Code) and the Bay Area Air Quality Management District building appliances rule page (updated November 12, 2026).

What the air district rule means for your purchase

Until the new compliance date, a replacement gas heater is bought the way it always has been. After it, the rule is meant to steer replacements toward zero-NOx models, with exemptions for qualifying low-income owners and for properties with significant electrical or space constraints, according to the district.

Ask the installer which models are allowed on the day of installation. Rules can change again, so check baaqmd.gov before you sign.

The swap, step by step

What happens on replacement day

  1. The gas control is turned off, the water supply is closed and the tank is drained to a floor drain or the yard.
  2. Gas, water, vent and relief valve connections are disconnected, and the old tank is carried out.
  3. The new tank is set on its platform or pan, strapped at the top and bottom thirds and checked for level.
  4. New flex lines, a shutoff, a sediment trap, a relief valve and its discharge pipe are installed, plus an expansion tank where needed.
  5. Gas joints are leak tested, the burner is lit, the vent draft is checked and the inspector is scheduled.

Venting and combustion air

A gas tank vents through a flue that must rise continuously to the outdoors with the clearance the maker requires. The Pleasanton handout calls for 6 inches of clearance for single-wall vent and 1 inch for B-vent, and a vent cap at least one foot above the roof.

A closet needs combustion air openings near the floor and the ceiling. A new heater in an old closet that was sealed up in a past remodel is a common problem, and we check it before the old tank comes out.

Maintenance that extends the life of the new tank

Flush the tank once a year to remove sediment that insulates the burner and makes the heater work harder. Test the relief valve by lifting its lever briefly, with a bucket under the discharge pipe, and replace it if it does not reseat. Check the anode rod every two or three years, since it corrodes first so the tank does not.

Set the thermostat to 120 degrees Fahrenheit for a balance of comfort, safety and efficiency. Keep the area around the heater clear of stored items, since a gas heater needs air and clearance to burn safely.
