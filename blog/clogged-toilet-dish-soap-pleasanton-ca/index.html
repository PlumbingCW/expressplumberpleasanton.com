Clogged Toilet Dish Soap Trick: A Pleasanton, CA Homeowner Guide

Dish soap and hot water can free a soft clog in a toilet, and it costs almost nothing to try. Here is how to do it safely, how to tell when it will not work and what to do next.

By the Pleasanton Express Plumbing teamUpdated November 13, 20267 min read

Yes, dish soap can unclog a toilet when the blockage is soft, such as too much paper or waste. The soap coats the pipe wall and lets the clog slide, and hot water helps loosen it. The method takes about half an hour and works best on a slow toilet that still drains a little.

It will not clear a solid object, a main line stoppage or a clog caused by a wipe that has wrapped around itself. If you try it and nothing changes, stop and call a plumber instead of flushing again.

Why dish soap sometimes works

A toilet drains through a curved passage called the trap. When the trap is partly blocked with paper and waste, water sits high in the bowl and moves out slowly. Liquid dish soap is a lubricant, and it can coat the clog and the pipe wall so the blockage slides through when water pushes on it.

Hot water adds a second effect. It softens toilet paper and organic waste, which breaks the clog into smaller pieces. The key word is hot, not boiling. Boiling water can crack a porcelain bowl, and a cracked bowl is a replacement, not a repair.

The dish soap method, step by step

  1. Stop flushing and take the tank lid off. If the bowl is near the rim, close the supply valve behind the toilet so the tank cannot refill.
  2. Squeeze about a quarter cup of liquid dish soap into the bowl, and let it sit for fifteen to twenty minutes so it reaches the clog.
  3. Heat a gallon of water until it is hot to the touch but not boiling, then pour it from waist height so the force helps push the clog.
  4. Wait ten to fifteen minutes. If the level drops, flush once to test.
  5. If the bowl is still high, use a flange plunger next, with steady strokes and the seal held tight.

Plunger and closet auger before you call

A flange plunger has a rubber extension that seals the toilet drain. Push down gently first to push out the air, then pull and push steadily for about twenty seconds. A flat sink plunger will not seal and wastes effort.

A closet auger reaches farther than a plunger and has a protective sleeve so it does not scratch the bowl. Feed it in, crank gently and pull out whatever it hooks. If you meet a hard stop, do not force it, because you may be pushing an object deeper.

Pleasanton permit rules and a clogged toilet

The City of Pleasanton's construction permit handout exempts several jobs that homeowners and plumbers do every week. It lists clearing stoppages and stopping leaks in drains and pipes, and it also lists removing and reinstalling a toilet, as long as no valves, pipes or fixtures are replaced or rearranged.

In practical terms, clearing a clog needs no permit in Pleasanton, and pulling a toilet to reset it generally does not either. Swapping in a new toilet is different, and the city's bathroom handout lists 1.28 gallons per flush for toilets, so ask the Building and Safety Division if your project goes beyond a reset.

Signs the clog is in the main line

A toilet is the lowest-priced fixture to unclog, but it is not always the problem. If a toilet and a shower both drain slowly, or if water bubbles in one fixture when another drains, the blockage is probably in the main drain. A plunger cannot reach it.

In that case, stop using water so the line does not back up into the lowest drain in the house. A plumber can clear it with a cable or hydro jetting, and a camera shows whether roots or a damaged pipe caused it. Our drain cleaning page explains the options.

What to keep out of a Pleasanton toilet

A short flush rule for the whole household

  • Only toilet paper and human waste should be flushed.
  • Wipes labeled flushable do not break down like paper, and they are a leading cause of repeat clogs.
  • Paper towels, tissues, cotton swabs and dental floss belong in the trash.
  • Feminine products, diapers and cat litter should never be flushed.
  • Use less paper per flush, and flush twice rather than once when you need more.
