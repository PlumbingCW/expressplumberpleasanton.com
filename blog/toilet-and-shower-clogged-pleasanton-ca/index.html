Toilet and Shower Both Clogged? A Pleasanton, CA Fix Guide

One clogged fixture is a nuisance. Two at once is a sign that the blockage is farther down the line, and the right first move is to stop adding water.

Call (510) 544-1962
By the Pleasanton Express Plumbing teamUpdated November 13, 20267 min read

When a toilet and a shower clog at the same time, the blockage is almost always downstream of both, in the branch line or the main drain. A plunger will not reach it, and chemical drain cleaners rarely help. The best response is to stop adding water and to find out where the problem starts.

This guide helps you work out which line is blocked, what to try and what to avoid. It ends with how a plumber clears the line and how to tell whether the issue is yours or the city's.

Which line is blocked?

A quick way to narrow it down.
What happensWhere the blockage probably is
Only the toilet is slowThe toilet trap or its own drain.
Toilet and nearby tub or shower are slowThe bathroom branch line.
Toilet flush makes the shower or tub gurgle or fillThe branch line or the main.
Lowest drain in the house backs up when you run the washerThe main line.
Every fixture is slow and a floor drain or cleanout shows waterThe main line or the lateral to the street.

The lowest drain in the house, often a shower, tub or floor drain on the ground floor, is where a blocked main shows itself first. Water has to go somewhere, and it finds the lowest opening.

What to do in the first ten minutes

Stop it from getting worse

  1. Stop flushing, showering, running the washer and using the dishwasher.
  2. Check the cleanout, a capped pipe in the yard or garage. If it is full of water, the blockage is farther down toward the street.
  3. Do not pour chemical cleaners into a backed-up fixture, since they sit in the standing water and are hard on pipes.
  4. Move rugs and valuables from the lowest floor, and cover floor drains with a heavy mat if you can.
  5. Call a plumber and tell them every fixture that is affected.

What a plumber does to clear it

A plumber runs a drum cable machine through a cleanout or a pulled toilet and cuts through roots, paper and debris. For heavy grease or roots on the pipe wall, hydro jetting scours the pipe. After the line is clear, a camera shows whether a break or a sag caused the problem.

If it was a one-time blockage, the repair is done. If roots or a belly in the pipe are the cause, the stoppage will return, and sewer line repair is the longer-term fix. Our sewer line cleaning page covers the process.

Common causes in older and newer homes

Why a main drain stops

  • Tree roots entering joints in clay, cast iron or older plastic laterals.
  • A sagging section, called a belly, that holds water and waste.
  • Grease, paper and wipes that collect in a low spot.
  • A cracked or offset joint that lets soil and roots in.
  • A collapsed section after years of corrosion.

Homes near Downtown Pleasanton are older and are more likely to have clay or cast iron laterals with joints that roots can enter. Newer areas such as Callippe Preserve usually have plastic pipe, where a stoppage is more often from paper and wipes. A camera tells you which you have.

Preventing a repeat

Keep wipes and grease out of the drain, even when the package says flushable. Use a mesh hair catcher in showers. If your lateral is old and mature trees are nearby, a camera inspection every few years shows roots before they block the line.

Know where your cleanout is, and keep it accessible. A visible, capped cleanout cuts a plumber's time in half, and it cuts your bill. See sewer camera inspection for the process.

What not to do while you wait

Common mistakes with a main line backup

  • Do not plunge a toilet or tub that is backing up from the main, since the blockage is not at the fixture.
  • Do not put a small snake into a tub drain and hope to reach the main.
  • Do not pour boiling water or chemicals into a backed-up fixture.
  • Open a cleanout slowly and stand to the side, since sewage can spill out under pressure.
  • Do not keep running water to see whether it is getting better.

If sewage reaches the floor, keep children and pets away, wear gloves and boots, and ventilate. Contaminated water on carpet and drywall needs professional drying and cleaning, and your insurer will want photos.
