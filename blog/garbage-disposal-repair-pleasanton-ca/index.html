Kitchen Garbage Disposal Repair: A Pleasanton, CA Homeowner Guide

Most disposal problems are a jam, a tripped reset or a loose seal, and many can be fixed in ten minutes. Here is the safe way to diagnose it and what to do next.

Call (510) 544-1962
By the Pleasanton Express Plumbing teamUpdated November 13, 20268 min read

A garbage disposal that hums but does not spin is jammed, and one that makes no sound at all has usually tripped its reset button. A disposal that leaks has a failed seal, a loose flange or a cracked housing. Each problem has a different fix, and the first job is to find which one you have.

Never put your hand in the unit, even when it is off. Use tongs, pliers and a flashlight to see inside, and switch off the power at the wall or the breaker before you do anything else.

What the symptom tells you

Reading the signs.
SymptomCauseFix

Drains slowly with the disposal offClogged trap or kitchen lineClear the line.

The safe reset, in order

  1. Switch the disposal off at the wall and unplug it under the sink if you can reach the cord.
  2. Look into the sink with a flashlight. Use tongs to remove any visible object, such as a bottle cap or a utensil.
  3. Place the hex key from the unit's box into the socket at the center of the bottom, and turn it back and forth until the flywheel moves freely.
  4. Press the red reset button on the bottom of the unit.
  5. Restore power, run cold water and test. If the unit trips again, stop and call.

Leaks and where to look

Wipe the unit dry, then run water and watch where the first drop appears. A leak at the top, where the sink meets the disposal, points to the flange putty or gasket. A leak at the hose that goes to the dishwasher points to a loose clamp. A leak from the bottom means the unit is done.

Do not wait on a leak, because water in the cabinet softens the base and grows mold. Place a bucket under it and put down towels if you have to run the sink before the repair.

Kitchen habits that keep a disposal working

What to feed it and what to skip

  • Run cold water before, during and for about fifteen seconds after use.
  • Feed small pieces gradually, not a full sink at once.
  • Keep grease, oil and fat out of the drain, since they coat the pipe.
  • Skip fibrous items such as celery, corn husks and artichoke leaves, and use coffee grounds sparingly.
  • Never put bones, pits, glass, metal or non-food items in.

Pleasanton's food scrap rules and your disposal

The City of Pleasanton's page on the state organics law says food and plant waste goes in the green cart and describes it as becoming compost. The same page says state law requires residents and businesses to keep food and other compostable materials out of landfills.

It does not mention garbage disposals, so the green cart is not a replacement for a disposal, but it is a good place for bulky, fibrous or greasy scraps that do not belong in your drain. Keeping those out of the sink reduces the load on the disposal and on your kitchen line.

Source type: City of Pleasanton garbage and recycling page on the SB 1383 compost and recycling law.

Repair or replace

A decision guide.
SituationBetter choice
Jam, tripped reset or loose connectionRepair.
Leak from the top flangeRepair if the housing is intact.
Leak from the housingReplace.
Unit over about ten years old and tripping oftenReplace.
Frequent clogs from a weak motorReplace with a higher-horsepower model.

A half horsepower unit suits light use, and three quarters to one horsepower suits a busy kitchen. The model should fit your sink mount, and it needs a switched outlet or a hardwired connection.

When the problem is the drain, not the disposal

If water backs up in the other sink when the disposal runs, the kitchen line is partly blocked. A new disposal on a blocked line will back up again. A plumber can clear the line with a cable or hydro jetting, and our drain cleaning page covers the process.

Our garbage disposal repair team checks the drain and the dishwasher connection on every visit, since both share the same trap.

What a replacement visit involves

A typical swap

  1. The power is switched off and the old unit is unhooked from the drain, the dishwasher hose and the electrical connection.
  2. The mounting ring is released, and the old unit is lifted out of the sink flange.
  3. The new flange is set with plumber's putty or a gasket, and the new unit is hung from the mounting ring.
  4. The discharge pipe, trap and dishwasher hose are reconnected, and the dishwasher knockout plug is removed if the unit is new.
  5. Power is restored, and the plumber runs water and checks every joint for drips.

A straightforward swap takes an hour or two. The surprises are an outlet that is not switched, a cabinet that is too tight for a larger unit or a drain that needs work. We check those before we quote.
