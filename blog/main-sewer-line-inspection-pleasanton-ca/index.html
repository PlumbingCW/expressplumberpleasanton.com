Main Sewer Line Inspection for Pleasanton, CA Homes

A sewer camera turns a guess into a picture. This guide covers when an inspection is worth it, what the footage reveals and how Pleasanton splits responsibility for the pipe.

Call (510) 544-1962
By the Pleasanton Express Plumbing teamUpdated November 13, 20268 min read

A main sewer line inspection uses a waterproof camera on a flexible cable that travels from a cleanout or a pulled toilet toward the street. It records roots, cracks, offsets, low spots and buildup, and it measures how far each defect sits from the entry point. The result is a video and a plain report.

The inspection is worth doing when you are buying a home, after a backup, when you see slow drains with no clear cause or when your house is old and mature trees stand nearby. It is not needed on every house every year.

What the camera can show

Defects and what they mean.
FindingWhat it meansUsual next step
Roots through a jointWater is escaping and roots are following itClear and monitor, or repair.
Offset jointPipes have shifted, and the lip catches paperSpot repair if it blocks flow.
Belly or sagA low spot holds water and wasteRepair if it causes repeat blockages.
Crack or breakThe pipe has failed in one placeSpot repair or lining.
Heavy scale or greaseBuildup narrows the pipeHydro jetting.
Clean and intact pipeNo action neededKeep the report on file.

The report is as useful for what it does not find. A clean, continuous pipe lets you skip repairs and shows a buyer that the line has been checked.

When an inspection pays for itself

Reasons to scope the line

  • You are buying a house, and the sewer lateral is older than thirty years.
  • You have had a backup or repeated slow drains.
  • You see lush green patches or wet ground in the yard along the line.
  • Large trees stand near the lateral and the house is older.
  • You are planning a remodel that adds fixtures to the existing line.

What an inspection involves

The visit, start to finish

  1. The plumber finds the cleanout, or pulls a toilet or uses a roof vent if none exists.
  2. The camera is fed through the pipe while a screen shows the view and a locator tracks distance and depth.
  3. Defects are noted with their distance and position, and the plumber marks the ground above important findings.
  4. You see the footage, and the plumber explains what each item means.
  5. A written report with photos is sent to you for your records.

Pleasanton's sewer system and who maintains which pipe

The City of Pleasanton's Sewer System Management Plan says sewer service laterals are owned by, and the responsibility of, the property owner. The city may provide maintenance on laterals in the public right of way as a courtesy where a cleanout exists, and it does not repair laterals on private property.

The plan describes about 250 miles of gravity sewers, root cutting in defined areas every two years, enhanced cleaning at 1, 3, 6 and 12 month intervals on problem sections of main, and a goal of televising the system every seven years. Those are the city's mains. Your lateral is not on that schedule, so a camera inspection is how you learn its condition.

Source type: City of Pleasanton 2019 Sewer System Management Plan update.

Reading the footage and the report

A good report names the material of the pipe, the length inspected, the location of each defect and your options. Cast iron often shows scale and rust, clay shows joint offsets and root intrusion, and plastic pipe shows sags or cracks from poor bedding.

Ask for the video as well as the PDF. If you are buying a home, the video lets your own plumber or inspector give a second opinion, and it helps you negotiate a repair credit.

Keep the footage after the visit, even when the news is good. A clean recording from this year is a baseline, and a second inspection in a few years shows whether roots or sags are moving. Store the file with your closing papers so the next owner can see it too.

Questions to ask before you hire an inspector

What a good inspection includes

  • Do you give me the full video and a written report, or only a summary?
  • Does the camera have a locator so you can mark depth and position above ground?
  • Will you note the distance of every defect from the cleanout?
  • What happens if the camera cannot pass a blockage or a break?
  • Is cleaning included if the pipe is dirty, or is that a separate charge?
  • Will you tell me plainly which findings need action and which do not?

The best inspectors recommend the least work the pipe needs. A report that lists every flaw with no ranking leaves you guessing, and a report that tells you what to repair now, what to watch and what to ignore saves money.

Ask the same questions of any company that offers to scope your line, including ours. We would rather you know what you are buying than discover it on the day.
