Emergency Water Line Repair in Pleasanton, CA

The water line from the meter to your house is buried and out of sight. We locate failures, explain trench and trenchless options and restore pressure without wrecking your yard.

Emergency water line repair in Pleasanton covers the pipe that carries water from the meter to your home. When it fails you may see a soggy patch that stays wet in dry weather, hear water at the meter, lose pressure or watch a bill jump. Close the main valve and call (510) 544-1962.

Who owns what: meter, line and house

In most places the utility owns the meter and the customer owns the pipe from the meter to the house. The City of Pleasanton operates the local water system, so ask its utility staff where responsibility changes at your address before you hire anyone. We help you identify the break and which side of the meter it is on.

Symptoms of a failing water line

What to watch for

  • A wet patch or sunken spot in the yard with no sprinkler nearby.
  • The meter moving when every fixture in the house is off.
  • Sudden low water pressure or sputtering at taps.
  • A noticeable rise in your water bill with no change in use.
  • Discolored or cloudy water after a pressure change.

If the meter keeps moving after you close the house main, the leak is on the buried line. Our leak detection page has the meter test step by step.

Trench or trenchless: choosing the repair

Methods compared.
MethodHow it worksTrade-offs
Spot repair by trenchDig to the break, replace a section and backfillLowest cost when the line is otherwise sound and easy to reach.
Full trench replacementExcavate the full run and lay new pipeDirect and reliable, but disturbs lawn, drives and walkways.
Pipe burstingA new pipe is pulled through as the old one is splitLittle digging, needs entry and exit pits.
RerouteA new line goes a different path to the houseUseful when the old route is blocked by structures.

The right method depends on the pipe material, depth, length and what sits above the line. We explain the options and the likely landscape disruption before you decide.

Pipe materials we find on older lines

Older service lines in Pleasanton may be galvanized steel or early copper, and many have been replaced with polyethylene or newer copper. Galvanized pipe corrodes from the inside and slowly narrows, which cuts pressure long before it leaks. Copper can pit when soil or water chemistry is harsh.

Replacement pipe should match your pressure and local code. We tell you which material we recommend and why.

Before anyone digs

Safe excavation basics

  1. Call 811, the USA North 811 service for Northern California, at least two working days before digging so buried utilities are marked.
  2. We locate the break with listening gear so the dig is as small as possible.
  3. Gas, electric, sewer and irrigation lines near the route are identified before trenching.
  4. We protect lawn, plants and hardscape where possible and tell you what will be disturbed.
  5. The new line is tested for pressure before the trench is backfilled.

The City of Pleasanton's permit handout says ordinary repairs do not include replacement or relocation of any water supply or sewer piping, so replacing a service line is permitted work. Stopping a leak in a pipe is exempt, but a concealed pipe that must be replaced counts as new work. We handle the permit and the inspection.

Pressure regulators and shutoff valves

Most homes have a pressure regulator near the main. If it fails, the full street pressure enters the house and fittings, hoses and the water heater suffer. We check the regulator and the main shutoff during a water line visit, since a shutoff that does not close is a problem in the next emergency.

A new main shutoff valve is a small cost that makes future repairs, including a burst pipe, far easier to control.
