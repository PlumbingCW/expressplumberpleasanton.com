Livermore homes by era, and what each one tends to need

Livermore was platted in 1869 as a railroad town, and the Livermore Valley wine region grew up on its south side from the 1880s. The city then roughly tripled in population between 1970 and 2000, so a large share of its homes date from those three decades. Downtown redevelopment is still under way, which means new buildings sit beside very old ones.

If a pipe has failed once, ask for an inspection of the rest. A leak detection visit finds the failing line before it floods a wall, and an old copper system with several pinholes is often cheaper to replace than to patch.

Who supplies your water in Livermore

Water in Livermore reaches homes through more than one retailer. Zone 7 Water Agency lists both California Water Service and the City of Livermore Water Resources Division among its four retailers. Cal Water's 2015 plan says its Livermore district covers about 48 percent of the city's incorporated area.

That plan also says Zone 7 purchased water supplied roughly 70 percent of the district's needs, with groundwater from the Mocho Subbasin supplying about 30 percent. Groundwater in this valley is harder than treated surface water, so scale on faucets and inside tanks varies from one street to the next.

Check which company bills you, read its annual water quality report and look at the inside of your water heater during a flush. Our water heater repair visits start with exactly that check.

Ground, rain and fault lines

Livermore averages about 14.6 inches of rain a year, mostly between November and April, and its valley soil is very gravelly. Gravel drains quickly, so a leaking pipe may not announce itself with a soggy yard. The meter test on our leak detection page is the best first check when a bill jumps.

The Greenville, Tesla and Livermore faults lie near the city. That is a reason to have the water heater strapped, to use flexible connectors and to know where the main shutoff is before you need it.

Before we arrive in Livermore

STEP 1

Find and close the main

Turn the main water valve clockwise until it stops. If you cannot find it, close the valve at the meter box.

STEP 2

Cut power near water

Switch off breakers for wet rooms or the water heater, only if you can reach the panel without standing in water.

STEP 3

Open a low tap

Open the lowest faucet in the house to drain pressure, and clear the area so we can reach the fixture.

Call and describe it
